## Why the two headline numbers were never comparable

| | team3 | this repo |
|---|---|---|
| Users with >= 1 positive | 171,728 | 171,728 |
| Split | 70 / 15 / 15 | 80 / 10 / 10 |
| Test users | 25,760 | **17,174** |
| Candidates per query (mean / median) | 9.17 / 5 | **89.16 / 90** |
| Ratio positives : negatives | 1:4 (resampled) | natural slate, 97.7% negative |
| NDCG cut-off actually used | **K = 5** (printed as `@10`) | K = 10 |

- `Data Preparation/1. Sampling.ipynb` keeps all positives and draws
  `4 * n_pos` negatives per query, so 61% of their slates have <= 5 items and
  81% have <= 10 items. Ranking the whole slate therefore already gives
  `Hit Rate ~ 1.0` (they report 0.9782).
- `17,174` is **our** test count (`171,728 * 0.10`), not a number from their repo.

## Takeaway

On team3's own 1:4 protocol our re-ranker scores **NDCG@5 0.7849 vs their
0.5684 (+0.217)** and `P@1 0.6955`. The full-slate `NDCG@10 = 0.4538/0.4596`
is the harder, leakage-free number and is what the report should quote as the
main result; the 1:4 number belongs in the "compared with prior work" row.
